2. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation

2.1 The Humanitarian Challenge in Post-Disaster Relief

Natural catastrophes—such as severe tropical cyclones, extreme riverine floods, devastating earthquakes, and tsunamis—inflict sudden, catastrophic damage on residential dwellings, small businesses, and community infrastructure. In the immediate wake of such events, governments and humanitarian disaster management organizations (e.g., NDMA, SDRF, FEMA) face the enormous challenge of rapidly surveying thousands of damaged properties, validating claimant eligibility, quantifying physical destruction, and calculating equitable, timely financial relief.

Traditional post-disaster damage assessment methodologies suffer from fundamental operational deficiencies:

  • Excessive Operational Latency: Manual on-site surveys take weeks or months to reach inundated or physically cut-off disaster zones, causing acute distress to affected families awaiting basic relief funds.
  • Subjectivity & Inconsistency: Field inspections conducted by varied teams under grueling conditions produce inconsistent loss estimates, lack standardized objective evidence, and invite public disputes.
  • Vulnerability to Fraud & Leakage: Paper-based claims without pre-disaster photographic baselines create significant opportunities for duplicate claims, inflated damage declarations, and fraudulent payouts.
  • Opaque Decision-Making: Claimants are frequently left uninformed regarding how relief amounts were computed, undermining citizen trust in official relief administration.

2.2 The DisasterAid Solution

DisasterAid resolves these systemic challenges by delivering an integrated, full-stack, enterprise-grade AI decision-support platform. It unites satellite/drone remote sensing deep learning with municipal-level citizen property records, automated multi-temporal image change detection, and a transparent, deterministic statutory compensation policy engine.

1. Citizen Baseline Property Registry Proactive

Citizens securely register property assets with GPS coordinates and high-resolution pre-disaster baseline imagery prior to catastrophe onset, creating an immutable physical baseline.

2. AI Remote Sensing & Vision BDANet DL

Multiscale dual-branch neural network performs pre- vs post-disaster image comparison, structural change detection, 128-D vector embedding similarity, and 4-tier severity categorization.

3. Deterministic Policy 2026.1 Statutory Rule

Separates physical damage ratios from financial relief math. Applies transparent statutory coverage caps and deductibles to generate provisional compensation recommendations.

4. Human-in-the-Loop Audit Portal Governance

Strict statutory compliance safeguards prevent autonomous fund transfers. Official review officers inspect photographic evidence side-by-side, verify AI metrics, and record audited decisions.

2.3 Ethical AI Principles & Human-in-the-Loop Safeguards

Statutory Safeguard Notice: Provisional Decision Support Only

DisasterAid is strictly designed as an objective decision-support accelerator. Under Policy 2026.1, all AI-generated damage scores and compensation figures are explicitly labeled as Provisional Recommendations. Automatic disbursement of funds without verified administrative officer review and documented justification is technically blocked and prohibited by system policy.

3. Base Paper Foundation: BDANet Deep Learning Architecture

3.1 Academic Citation & Research Scope

The core computer vision and damage assessment engine in DisasterAid is directly grounded in the state-of-the-art research published in:

Base Research Paper

Paper: BDANet: Multiscale Convolutional Neural Network With Cross-Directional Attention for Building Damage Assessment From Satellite Images
Authors: Y. Shen, S. Zhu, T. Yang, and C. Chen
Journal: IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing (IEEE TGRS), Vol. 60, Art no. 5402114, pp. 1-14, 2022.
Benchmark Dataset: xBD Large-Scale Satellite Building Damage Dataset (xView2 Challenge).

3.2 Key Architectural Innovations in BDANet

Traditional building damage assessment networks simply concatenate pre-disaster and post-disaster satellite images into a single multi-channel tensor. This naive approach suffers from two severe limitations: (1) it fails to capture cross-temporal directional relationships, and (2) subtle structural damage classes ("No Damage" vs. "Minor Damage") exhibit severe spectral overlap and class imbalance. BDANet overcomes these issues through four major architectural innovations:

1. Two-Stage Convolutional Framework with Siamese Weight Sharing

BDANet decouples the complex task of damage assessment into two sequential stages:

  • Stage 1 (Building Localization U-Net): Takes the pre-disaster image as input and outputs a high-fidelity binary building footprint mask (P_B in {0, 1}), isolating building structures from natural terrain, water bodies, and roads.
  • Siamese Weight Sharing: The convolutional feature extractor weights learned in Stage 1 are transferred and shared between the two parallel branches (pre-disaster and post-disaster) of the Stage 2 Siamese encoder. This weight-sharing strategy guarantees feature space alignment and accelerates convergence.
  • Stage 2 (Damage Assessment Network): Uses the Siamese encoders to process multi-temporal image pairs, feeds representations through Cross-Directional Attention, and outputs a 4-tier damage classification map (P_d). The final prediction is given by the pixel-wise product: P = argmax(P_B * P_d).

2. Multiscale Feature Fusion (MFF) Module

Satellite images contain buildings with vastly differing spatial dimensions, ranging from compact rural sheds (5x5 pixels) to large industrial complexes (150x150 pixels). The MFF module extracts and fuses multi-level feature maps across three scales (1x, 0.5x, 0.25x), ensuring that both fine-grained edge details and wide spatial context are simultaneously preserved.

3. Cross-Directional Attention (CDA) Mechanism

To capture directional inter-dependencies across time, BDANet introduces two complementary cross-directional attention modules:

  • Channel Cross-Attention (I_cha): Evaluates channel-wise correlation between pre- and post-disaster feature maps, reweighting informative feature channels that signal destruction.
  • Spatial Cross-Attention (I_spa): Modulates spatial pixel locations based on structural disparity, highlighting damaged rooftops and collapsed walls while suppressing unchanged background areas.

4. Class-Specific CutMix Augmentation

In catastrophe datasets, "Destroyed" or "No Damage" buildings predominate, whereas "Minor Damage" and "Major Damage" instances are severely underrepresented. BDANet applies Class-Specific CutMix by selectively cutting localized building patches from minority damage categories and pasting them onto diverse training pairs, effectively neutralizing severe class imbalance.

3.3 Mathematical Formulation & Objective Functions

The complete network is optimized using compound loss formulations that balance pixel localization accuracy and class-balanced classification:

Stage 1 Localization Loss:   L_loc = L_BCE(P_B, Y_B) + L_Dice(P_B, Y_B)

Where L_BCE is standard Binary Cross Entropy evaluating pixel footprint likelihood, and L_Dice measures the overlap ratio between ground-truth and predicted building contours:

L_Dice = 1 - (2 * sum(P_B * Y_B) + epsilon) / (sum(P_B) + sum(Y_B) + epsilon)
Stage 2 Damage Assessment Loss:   L_dmg = alpha * L_CE(P_d, Y_d) + beta * L_Dice(P_d, Y_d)

Evaluation follows the rigorous official xView2 Challenge Benchmark Scoring Standard:

Damage F1 (Harmonic Mean):   F1_d = 4 / ( (1 / F1_NoDmg) + (1 / F1_Minor) + (1 / F1_Major) + (1 / F1_Destroyed) )
Overall Evaluation Metric:   F1_s = 0.3 * F1_b + 0.7 * F1_d

Where F1_b is the Building Localization F1 score, and individual class F1 scores represent No Damage, Minor Damage, Major Damage, and Destroyed tiers respectively.

Why Harmonic Mean Matters in Disaster Relief

Unlike standard arithmetic averages which allow high accuracy on majority classes (No Damage) to mask total failure on minority classes, the harmonic mean heavily penalizes low scores in any single category. A model that fails on "Minor Damage" receives a near-zero F1_d score, ensuring genuine multi-class damage classification rigor.

5.2 High-Level Claim Lifecycle Workflow

The complete claim lifecycle is divided into seven structured phases guaranteeing end-to-end auditability and strict policy compliance:

Phase 1: Citizen Onboarding & Property Baseline Registration

An applicant creates an authenticated citizen profile and records property assets with GPS coordinates and high-resolution pre-disaster baseline imagery. This pre-disaster record establishes an undeniable physical baseline.

Phase 2: Post-Catastrophe Claim Ingestion

Following an incident, the applicant files a formal claim, specifying catastrophe category (Flood, Cyclone, Earthquake, Tsunami), incident date, and uploading multiple post-disaster damage photographs.

Phase 3: Secure File Validation & Storage

FastAPI and Pillow validate image integrity, verify dimensions, enforce file size bounds (10MB max), reject corrupted files, and persist photos into isolated disk directories.

Phase 4: Verified Benchmark Dataset Matching

The system identifies geographic reference zones (e.g. Cuddalore, Chennai, Kerala) and queries 40 verified benchmark pairs for visual similarity retrieval.

Phase 5: AI Feature Extraction & Change Detection

The AI engine computes 128-D normalized embeddings, evaluates Structural Similarity (SSIM), Normalized Cross-Correlation (NCC), and assigns a 4-tier damage classification.

Phase 6: Deterministic Statutory Compensation Modeling

Policy 2026.1 calculates gross estimated loss, deductibles, and provisional compensation, generating an explainable audit record.

Phase 7: Administrative Adjudication

An authorized Review Officer accesses the Admin Portal, reviews photographic evidence side-by-side, inspects AI metrics, and records an immutable binding decision.

6. Computer Vision & Feature Embedding Engine

6.1 128-Dimensional Multi-Scale Feature Embedding Architecture

To enable instantaneous semantic similarity search against verified disaster benchmarks, DisasterAid implements a deterministic, multi-scale visual feature extractor producing a 128-dimensional L2-normalized vector embedding for every uploaded image:

1. Spatial Grid Pooling (48 Dims) Color & Layout

The 256x256 RGB image is partitioned into a 4x4 spatial grid (16 equal patches). The mean pixel intensity across each color channel (R, G, B) is pooled: 16 * 3 = 48 dimensions, encoding spatial color layout.

2. Edge & Gradient Energy (32 Dims) Structural Edges

Sobel-like finite difference operators compute spatial gradient magnitude sqrt(dx^2 + dy^2). Mean and standard deviation are pooled across a 4x4 grid: 16 * 2 = 32 dimensions, capturing structural boundaries.

3. Statistical Moments (8 Dims) Global Texture

Evaluates global luminance, variance, and contrast moments across color channels, representing lighting balance and surface texture distributions (8 dimensions).

4. 2D FFT Spectral Analysis (40 Dims) Frequency Bands

Applies a 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT2) with center-shifted frequency spectra. Center-band frequencies are downsampled to 40 dimensions, capturing debris granularity and rubble patterns.

6.2 Vector Normalization & Cosine Similarity Search

The combined 128-dimensional vector v is projected onto the unit hypersphere via L2 normalization:

e = v / ||v||_2 = v / sqrt(sum(v_i^2))

Cosine similarity between claimant photograph embedding e_c and reference image embedding e_r is evaluated and scaled to [0.0, 1.0]:

Sim(e_c, e_r) = (1 + cos(e_c, e_r)) / 2 = (1 + (e_c . e_r)) / 2

7. Multi-Temporal Change Detection & Damage Severity Classification

7.1 Change Detection Pipeline & Noise Filtering

Accurate damage assessment requires distinguishing genuine physical destruction from sensor noise, varying solar illumination angles, and camera compression artifacts. The pipeline executes five rigorous steps:

  1. Byte & Array Identity Check: If baseline and damaged photographs are identical (zero difference), the engine immediately flags 0.0 damage and 0.99 alignment confidence.
  2. Spatial Median Filtering: Applies a 3x3 median filter to eliminate high-frequency salt-and-pepper sensor noise and JPEG block compression artifacts.
  3. Structural Similarity Index (SSIM): Evaluates local 8x8 window luminance, contrast, and structural texture preservation.
  4. Normalized Cross-Correlation (NCC): Measures global linear alignment and correlation between pre- and post-disaster grayscale arrays.
  5. Coherent Structural Disparity & Edge Destruction: Detects severe pixel intensity shifts (Delta I > 25 and Delta I > 50) and computes directional edge loss.
Composite Damage Formulation:
D_raw = 0.35 * (1 - max(0, NCC)) + 0.35 * (1 - SSIM) + 0.30 * min(1.0, 6.0 * P_sig + 10.0 * P_sev)

7.2 4-Tier Damage Severity Classification

The composite damage score (0.0 <= D <= 1.0) is mapped to standardized xBD severity tiers:

Severity Tier Score Range Physical Structural Characteristics Default Policy Multiplier
No Damage D < 0.08 Completely intact structure; zero visible roof or wall disruption; hairline surface paint weathering only. 0.0% (Zero loss)
Minor Damage 0.08 <= D < 0.30 Localized roof tile displacement, non-load-bearing window breakage, cosmetic water staining. 10.0% to 30.0%
Major Damage 0.30 <= D < 0.65 Partial roof collapse, substantial structural wall fissures, foundation shifting, non-functional. 35.0% to 70.0%
Destroyed D >= 0.65 Irreparable structural collapse, rubble field, complete roof detachment, foundation obliterated. 75.0% to 100.0%

7.3 Uncertainty Flagging & Limitation Reporting

To support responsible administrative oversight, the AI engine evaluates alignment confidence and raises explicit uncertainty flags when data quality falls below acceptable thresholds:

  • Low Alignment Confidence: Triggered if photographic correlation NCC < 0.50, indicating differing camera viewing perspectives or severe obstruction.
  • Cloud, Smoke, or Shadow Obstruction: Extreme intensity drops across large regions trigger occlusion warnings.
  • Off-Nadir Satellite Distortion: Extreme perspective angles that skew building footprints are flagged in the assessment notes.
Uncertainty Mitigation Protocol

When an uncertainty flag is raised, the system displays an amber warning banner in the Admin Workbench. The review officer is prompted to either request additional ground-level photographs from the applicant or dispatch a localized manual inspection team before finalizing compensation.

8. Explainable Statutory Compensation Policy Engine (Policy 2026.1)

8.1 Policy Charter & Guiding Principles

Financial compensation in DisasterAid is governed strictly by the National Disaster Relief & Property Assistance Policy (Version 2026.1). The policy is built upon three foundational tenets:

  • Strict Decoupling of Visual Similarity from Financial Math: Visual similarity scores indicate evidentiary confidence; they never influence dollar/rupee relief calculations.
  • Deterministic Rule-Based Math: Payout recommendations follow explicit mathematical statutory formulas, ensuring full explainability and eliminating model hallucination.
  • Statutory Caps & Progressive Deductibles: Relief focuses on basic restoration and recovery, subject to statutory limits aligned with state NDRF/SDRF frameworks.

8.2 Property Types & Statutory Coverage Limits

Maximum relief limits are enforced based on declared property category:

Property Category Statutory Coverage Limit Statutory Ceiling Description
Residential Rs 25,00,000 (Rs 25 Lakhs) Individual dwellings, urban apartments, rural homesteads
Commercial Rs 50,00,000 (Rs 50 Lakhs) Retail shops, warehouses, small & medium commercial establishments
Agricultural Rs 15,00,000 (Rs 15 Lakhs) Farm buildings, storage sheds, irrigation pump houses
Industrial Rs 75,00,000 (Rs 75 Lakhs) Manufacturing plants, processing facilities, heavy workshops

8.3 The Complete Compensation Calculation Formula

The policy engine calculates compensation through a transparent, itemized 4-step sequence:

Step 1: Gross Estimated Loss Computation

Gross loss is computed as the product of effective declared property value V_eff and the calibrated physical damage multiplier M:
Gross Estimated Loss = Round(Effective Declared Value * Damage Multiplier, 2)

Step 2: Statutory Deductible Application

To prevent nuisance claims and encourage private insurance coverage, a statutory deductible is applied:
Deductible = Min(Rs 50,000, Max(Rs 15,000, 5% of Gross Estimated Loss))

Step 3: Subtotal Net of Deductible

Subtotal After Deductible = Max(0.0, Gross Estimated Loss - Deductible)

Step 4: Statutory Coverage Cap Enforcement

Recommended Provisional Compensation = Min(Subtotal After Deductible, Maximum Coverage Limit)

12. Enterprise Security, Privacy & Compliance Architecture

12.1 Authentication & Password Security

User credentials are protected using the state-of-the-art Argon2id password hashing algorithm via the pwdlib security library, configured with memory-hard parameters to resist brute-force and GPU/ASIC cracking attacks. Authentication tokens are issued as cryptographically signed HMAC-SHA256 (HS256) JSON Web Tokens with strict 60-minute expiration windows.

12.2 Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) & Tenant Isolation

Critical Security Invariant: Public Privilege Escalation Defense

Public registration endpoints (/users/register) strictly provision only the applicant role. Administrative roles (admin) can never be acquired via public self-registration; they require out-of-band database seeding or official administrative provisioning.

Strict server-side tenancy boundary checks guarantee property and claim isolation:

  • Applicant Property Ownership Isolation: Applicants can only query, view, or submit claims for properties registered under their authenticated user ID. Any attempt to reference another citizen's property ID is intercepted with an immediate 403 Forbidden.
  • Administrative Endpoint Enforcement: All routes under /admin/* verify that current_user.role == 'admin'. Non-admin tokens are immediately rejected with 403 Forbidden.

12.3 Protected Media Streaming & Directory Traversal Defense

Images stored in the local file system (uploads/properties/, uploads/claims/) are never exposed via unauthenticated static file servers. Instead, they are streamed exclusively through the secure endpoint /files/{file_path}:

# Path Normalization & Traversal Mitigation
target_abs = (backend_dir / clean_incoming).resolve()
is_in_uploads = target_abs.is_relative_to((backend_dir / "uploads").resolve())
is_in_basepaper = target_abs.is_relative_to((workspace_dir / "basepaper").resolve())
if not (is_in_uploads or is_in_basepaper):
    raise HTTPException(status_code=403, detail="File access denied.")

12.4 Ethical AI Compliance & Responsible Governance

In adherence to international responsible AI standards:

  1. Human-in-the-Loop Supremacy: Under no circumstances can AI models authorize fund disbursements autonomously.
  2. Auditability & Non-Repudiation: Every administrative determination is recorded in the immutable claim_decisions audit log, preserving reviewer ID, timestamp, before/after status, and written justification.
  3. Data Minimization: Only photographs essential to assessing physical structural integrity are ingested; facial images and personally identifiable documents are excluded from vision processing.
